# DUENDE AT 174 — Flamenco × Liquid Drum & Bass
### 4/4 · 174 BPM · 320kbps · E Phrygian dominant · One vocalist (cantaora), one guitarist, one producer

**Fusion brief.** Flamenco and liquid drum & bass share more than they seem to. Both run on a repeating cycle that everyone in the room can feel before anyone counts it, both build tension by withholding the downbeat, and both pay off when the cycle finally lands. The tracks keeps the twelve-beat compás accents (3, 6, 8, 10, 12) as the rhythmic skeleton, and the breakbeat rolls underneath and around them. Nylon-string rasgueado and falseta stay acoustic and dry. The bass is a warm sub. Palmas replace the hi-hat top layer. The cante is sung straight, with melisma and no pitch correction.

**Suno Style (110 chars).** `flamenco x liquid drum and bass, 174 BPM, nylon guitar, palmas, rolling breakbeat, sub bass, cante jondo vocal`

**Suno Exclude field:** `trap hi-hats, EDM drop, autotune, metal, jazz, spoken word`

**Story & Context.** A night-shift baker in a port city hears two things through the wall of her flat: the guitar lesson downstairs and the drum machine upstairs. Neither neighbour knows the other exists. She starts to hum a line that fits both, and by morning the building is playing one song across three floors. Duende is the word for the moment a performance stops being performed, and the lyric keeps trying to name it without pinning it down.

**Visual & Lyrics Prompt.** A cross-section of a tenement building at 4 a.m., three lit windows: a guitarist in the bottom flat, a baker with flour on her hands in the middle, a producer at a glowing sampler on top. Cobalt and amber palette, warm tungsten light, faint motion trails on the hands, film grain.

**Structural Device.** The compás cycle never resets. Every section is a multiple of twelve counts (six bars at 174 BPM half-time feel), so the palmas accent pattern runs unbroken through the whole track. The drums enter one accent at a time: kick on 12 first, snare on 3 and 8 next, and the full break only at the second chorus. The drop is a *silence*, one full cycle of palmas alone, then the sub returns.

**Production notes**
- Keep the guitar dry and close-mic'd. Reverb belongs on the vocal and pad only.
- Sidechain the sub to the kick on count 12, not to every beat, so the compás accent is audible.
- Leave a real breath before the Palmas Break. The silence is the drop.
- Do not quantize the palmas fully; a few milliseconds of drag against the break gives the fusion its feel.

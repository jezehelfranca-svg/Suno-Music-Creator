# FOLLOW THE STENCH — Flamenco × Liquid Drum & Bass
### 4/4 · 174 BPM · 320kbps · E Phrygian dominant · One cantaor, one guitarist, one producer · Pirate-ship comedy

**Source.** Rewritten from a page of *The Secrets of the Ship of Secrets*: the narrator sighs, picks up a bucket and a scrubbing brush, and walks toward the source of a terrible smell below deck. It gets worse with every step. The page ends on the narrator's verdict that this is the worst thing they have ever smelled, with a wall of flies, mossy planks, a swinging lantern, two iron-banded doors and a wince of a face at the bottom. The lyric keeps the beats (the sigh, the tools, the approach, the worsening, the "what else could I do?") and adds nothing about what is behind the door, because the page doesn't say.

**Fusion brief.** Same engine as before: flamenco's 12-count compás accents (3, 6, 8, 10, 12) as the skeleton, a rolling liquid-DnB break around them. The comedy comes from playing a bad smell with the full gravity of cante jondo. The cantaor sings about a scrubbing brush as if it were a lost love. Nylon-string rasgueado stays dry. Palmas are the footsteps. The flies are the hi-hat layer: fast, papery, sixteenth-note shakers with a high-pass filter.

**Suno Style (113 chars).** `flamenco x liquid drum and bass, 174 BPM, nylon guitar, palmas, rolling breakbeat, sub bass, dramatic cante, comic`

**Suno Exclude field:** `trap hi-hats, EDM drop, autotune, metal, jazz, spoken word`

**Story & Context.** Below deck on an old ship, something has gone very wrong in the dark, and no one else is volunteering. The narrator sighs, takes the bucket and brush, and follows the smell along the corridor past the lanterns and the flies. Every verse is one door closer. The song stops with a hand on the latch, so the last chord is left unresolved.

**Visual & Lyrics Prompt.** A cramped ship corridor lit by two swinging lanterns, green with moss and barnacles, clouds of tiny flies drifting in the amber light, two heavy wooden doors banded in riveted iron with small square windows. A small anxious mouse in a pirate bandana holds a bucket and brush, nose wrinkled, eyes wide. Hand-inked storybook style, murky teal and lantern yellow, comic-book lettering for the words "BLECH!" and "STENCH!".

**Structural Device.** *Proximity.* One low-pass filter runs across the whole track, and it opens as the narrator approaches: muffled and distant in Verse 1, half open in Verse 2, fully open by the Final Chorus. Each verse is one door closer. The fly-shaker gets denser each section. The Palmas Break is the narrator stopping dead outside the door, palmas only, plus the bucket handle rattling in time. The final chord is *F* (the flat second), which never resolves to E. The door does not open.

**Production notes**
- Sing it completely straight. The joke works only if the cantaor commits to the drama.
- Automate one low-pass filter on the whole master bus across the track, closed at the top and open by the Final Chorus.
- Keep the guitar dry and close-mic'd. Reverb belongs on the vocal and pad only.
- Sidechain the sub to the kick on count 12, not every beat, so the compás accent stays audible.
- Leave a real breath before the Palmas Break, and record the bucket handle for real.
- End on F and let it ring. Do not resolve to E.

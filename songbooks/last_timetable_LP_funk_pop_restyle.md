# THE LAST TIMETABLE — *ONCE UPON A LONG AGO IN DRIVING RAIN* (Funk-Pop Restyle)
### 4/4 · 118 BPM · 320kbps · Funk-Pop with boy-group influence · Sung and rapped verses on every track

**Restyle brief.** The Saltwater Diner stories, the ten McCartney fusion titles and the cast (Sarah, Leo, Dev, the owner, the kids in the booth) all carry over from the Wind-Drift Folk edition. The sound is replaced: slap bass, clavinet, chicken-scratch guitar, disco strings and a horn section over a locked four-on-the-floor or funk backbeat, with bright pop melodies on the hooks. The woodwind DNA survives as the horn section, which is its natural funk translation, and the oboe line comes back as the flute hook on Track 3.

**Band restyle.** The Last Timetable becomes a four-voice group in the boy-group mould: two sung leads (bright tenor, warm baritone), two rappers (low gravel flow, quick melodic flow), all four on gang hooks and post-choruses. Verse 1 is sung, Verse 2 is rapped, bridges are sung (mostly falsetto) on every track.

**LP-wide Suno Exclude field:** `trap hi-hats, EDM drop, autotune, metal, acoustic ballad`

### Session Notes
- **Restyle logic.** Every story, title, character and core image from the Wind-Drift Folk edition is kept; the sonic world is replaced wholesale. The woodwind section translates to a horn section, which is what woodwinds become in funk; the oboe line survives as the Track 3 flute hook. The melancholy survives too — most of these are sad lyrics on bright grooves, reframed as the characters choosing to move rather than stand still.
- **Tempo lock.** All ten tracks sit at 118 BPM in 4/4, so the Wind-Drift ritardandos and breath-timed percussion are gone. Dynamics now come from arrangement: half-time verses and bridges (Tracks 3, 7, 10), subtraction (Track 8), density build (Track 7), voice-stacking (Tracks 1, 5), and one key change (Track 10).
- **Sung and rapped verses.** Every track has a sung Verse 1 and a rapped Verse 2 (8 bars, internal and end rhyme, no repeated lines); bridges are sung, mostly falsetto. Track 2 casts the rap as a character (Teddy Boy); Track 5 casts it as the bus driver; Track 6 makes it the letter Leo won't send.
- **Ad-lib audit.** The Wind-Drift breath notation and whisper doubles are replaced with funk interjections and gang post-chorus chants (*hey!*, *ooh*, *yeah*), which are genre-native here. No doo-wop scat anywhere.
- **Boy-group influence in Suno.** Suno rejects artist names in the style box, so the influence is carried by the descriptor "K-pop boy group" plus voice-trading structure, falsetto bridges and gang post-choruses.
- **320kbps.** Kept in the style string per the spec, but Suno doesn't take bitrate as a generation parameter. Export WAV and encode to 320kbps MP3 in the DAW.
- **Time anchors.** No clock times or weekdays. Durations and ages kept as substance: nine weeks, six weeks, two years, eleven days, nineteen, fourteen, four hundred steps.

### Suno DAW Flags
- **Track 8 subtraction.** Suno won't reliably drop instruments on cue. Generate full, then mute stems section by section in the DAW (horns at Verse 2, keys at Pre-Chorus 2, guitar at Chorus 2, everything but bass at the Bridge).
- **Track 7 density build.** Same issue in reverse: generate full and mute the synth bass, strings and horns until their entry sections.
- **Track 9 unresolved ending.** Suno will resolve to Bm. Cut the render at the last F#7#9 hit and let it ring or hard-stop.
- **Track 10 key change.** Suno may not modulate on command. Generate the final chorus as a separate clip in D♭ (or pitch-shift the C-major final chorus up one semitone) and splice at the A♭13 pivot.
- **Voice casting.** Suno can't assign four distinct singers. The [Verse 2: rap] and [Bridge: falsetto] tags steer it; for true voice-trading, generate sung and rapped sections as separate clips and comp them.
